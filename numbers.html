About 82,567

82,567 is a prime number: it has exactly two divisors, 1 and 82,567. Its neighbouring primes are 82,561 (six below) and 82,571 (four above). Its digits sum to 28, its digital root is 1, and in binary it is written 10100001010000111. Because it cannot be split into smaller whole factors, we use it as the symbol for this site: calculations you can’t argue with.

Why primes matter in business and AI

Prime numbers are the backbone of modern commerce. Public-key cryptography — the system that secures card payments, online banking and HTTPS — depends on the fact that multiplying two very large primes is easy, while factoring the result back is extraordinarily hard. Hash tables in databases often use prime-sized buckets to spread data evenly, and random-number generators used in simulations and AI training frequently rely on prime moduli.

Terms used above

Prime: exactly two divisors. Perfect: the sum of proper divisors equals the number (6, 28, 496, 8128). Abundant: that sum is larger. Deficient: that sum is smaller — every prime is deficient. Digital root: repeatedly sum digits until one digit remains.
